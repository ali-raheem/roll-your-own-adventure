Aurélien has watched every one of your bouts today. You know because you saw him, sitting on the
stairs with his notebook open on his knee, while you fenced Henrik and then Luca. Once, after you hit
Henrik on the toe, he wrote something down and underlined it.

He has no obvious style. He doesn't wait like the Heron or charge like the Hornet. He just seems to ask
questions with his blade, and wait for your answers.

Maître Colette is quiet for a long time. "He fences in second intention," she says eventually. "Every
action he shows you is a question. The attack you see is not the attack. Do not answer his questions.
Ask your own."

@position Period 1 · middle distance

*Allez.*

He comes forward in measured steps, and halfway across the piste he throws a half-lunge at your arm. It
stops short, deliberately. Then another. Each time his blade is in exactly the right place for a parry,
if you happen to counterattack.

## Taking the Bait {#f-bait}
@points you 1 A counter that got through
@points them 4 Parry-ripostes
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You counter to his arm as he half-lunges. His half-lunge was never an attack: his blade was waiting for
yours. He parries your counter, sweeping it out of line, and ripostes to your shoulder in one smooth
movement. Again. Again. Again. You get through once, when he's a fraction slow.

:::coach
That's *second intention*: a false attack designed to draw your counterattack, so he can parry it and
riposte. The action he showed you was only there to make you move.
:::

@learn BAIT | Aurélien's half-lunges are false attacks. He wants your counterattack so he can parry and riposte. Don't take the bait.

## Not Answering {#f-patience}

@points them 2 A real attack you didn't expect
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You don't react. His half-lunges fall short, and you don't bite. He tries a slightly longer one, and you
retreat half a step and let it die in the air. He looks at you for a moment through his mask.

Then, twice, one of the half-lunges turns into a full one, and he scores. You catch him once coming too close.

:::coach
Refusing to answer his question isn't a plan, but it's a start. He has to change something now.
:::

## Third Intention {#f-third}
@points you 4 Deceived his parry
@points them 1 A real attack
@position Period 1 · middle distance

He half-lunges. You start a counterattack, but only half-heartedly, your arm only half extended, and he
takes the bait you're offering: his parry sweeps across to collect your blade. It isn't there. You've
already dropped your point around his parry and extended to his arm, while his blade is still busy with
the space you left.

Red lamp. You do it again. He stands still for a moment after the second one.

:::coach
You answered his question with a question of your own. His second intention depends on your
counterattack being real; show him a false one and his parry becomes the opening. Some coaches call
this *third intention*: counter-time against counter-time.
:::

## Broken Rhythm {#f-rhythm}
@points you 3 Sudden attacks
@points them 2 He read one of them
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You advance slowly, even slower than him, step, pause, step. Then with no warning you explode. The
first time, he's still setting up his next question when your point lands. It works three times. Twice
he reads the change and parries.

:::coach
A second-intention fencer needs a steady rhythm to set his traps. When you change the tempo, he has to
guess, and that's a much worse game for him.
:::

@learn TEMPO | Aurélien needs a steady rhythm to set his traps. Change pace suddenly (slow, slow, then very fast) and he has to guess.
