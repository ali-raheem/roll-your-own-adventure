## The Capital Open

You came through the morning pools seeded sixth: four wins, two losses, and one bout you'd rather not
talk about. Now it's the direct elimination tables, and the top eight are left. Three more wins and the
gold is yours.

You fence épée, right-handed, pistol grip. Your coach, **Maître Colette**, has been doing this for forty
years and communicates mainly through eyebrows.

### The weapon

- **Épée** scores with the point only, and the **whole body** is target: mask, hand, foot, all of it.
- There is **no right of way**. Whoever hits first scores. If both hit within about a twenty-fifth of a
  second, both lights come on: a **double touch**, and **both fencers score**.
- So the hand and forearm, the closest target, decide most bouts, and the scoreboard matters: when you're
  ahead, doubles bring you closer to 15 without closing the gap.

### The bout

- Direct elimination bouts are to **15 touches**, or three periods of **three minutes** with a one-minute
  break between them. The moment someone reaches 15, it's over.
- A double touch at **14–14** doesn't count; you fence the last touch again.
- If the scores are level at time, there's one extra minute. Priority is drawn by lot, the first touch
  wins, and if nobody scores, the fencer with priority wins.
- A **yellow card** is a warning. A **red card** gives your opponent a touch. A **black card** is exclusion
  from the tournament. Refusing to salute is a black card.
- [[Retreat]] with both feet over the end of the piste and your opponent scores a touch.
- A minute of fencing with nobody really trying earns a **P-card** for passivity, given to whoever is
  behind (both fencers if level). Enough of them become penalty touches.

### How this works

Each choice covers a stretch of the bout: several exchanges, several touches. Choose well and you pull
ahead. Choose badly and you'll watch the other fencer's lamp light up again and again.

Every opponent here has a style, a habit and a weakness. You'll lose some bouts finding them. That's
what the scouting report is for.
