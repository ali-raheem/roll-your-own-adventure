You watched Henrik's last pool bout from the stairs. He barely moved. He stood at the end of his reach
with his arm almost straight and his point floating at his opponent's wrist, and every time the other
fencer came forward, the green lamp lit up. His opponent looked exhausted. Henrik looked bored.

He fences with a French grip, the long straight handle held near the pommel, which adds a few more
centimetres to a reach that didn't need them. And he's [[left-handed]], so everything you drill against
right-handers is the wrong way round.

Maître Colette plugs in your body wire. "He will wait," she says. "He is a heron. He stands in the
water until the fish swims to him. Don't be the fish."

## Manners {#h-salute}
@position Period 1 · on the en-garde lines

You pull your mask on. The referee stops everything and points at you, then at Henrik, then makes a
small, very pointed gesture with her weapon hand.

You salute. Henrik salutes back with the faintest smile.

:::rule
The salute to your opponent, the referee and the public isn't optional. Refusing it is a black card:

:::

@learn Always salute. Refusing is a black card, and the referee isn't joking.

## Long Distance {#h-p1a}

*En garde. Prêts? Allez!*

Henrik doesn't move. He settles on his line with his arm three quarters extended, point floating level

- [Explode into a long lunge at his chest](#h-lunge)
- [Stay out of distance and fish for his wrist with little extensions](#h-fish)

## The Long Lunge {#h-lunge}
@points you 1 Chest, once, when he was late
@points them 4 Stop-hits to your arm
@position Period 1 · long distance

You launch. It's a beautiful [[lunge]], fully extended, the kind Maître Colette makes you drill until your
thighs shake. And halfway there his green lamp is already on. His point has landed on your forearm,
inside your bell guard, and your blade arrives a clear beat later.

You try it three more times. You get him once, when he's slow to extend. The other three, his arm is
waiting for your arm.

:::coach
Épée has no right of way. Against a long reach, a long attack gives him the whole length of your arm to
hit before your point arrives. His *[[stop-hit]]* on your wrist isn't a counterattack in time. It's just
closer.
:::

@learn Long simple attacks into Henrik's reach get stop-hit on the arm. His point is waiting for your wrist.

- [Keep fencing](#h-p1b)

## Fishing {#h-fish}
@points you 1 Wrist
@points them 3 Wrist, longer reach
@position Period 1 · long distance

You stay out of [[distance]] and fish: little extensions at his wrist, toes on the edge of range. He fishes
back. It's the most épée thing in the world and it's a game of centimetres, and he has twenty more of
them than you do.

But you notice something while you're counting centimetres. Every time he reaches for your wrist, his
front foot slides forward first, then stays planted while his arm extends. A big left foot, sitting
there on the steel.

@learn FOOT | When Henrik reaches for your wrist, his front foot slides forward first and stays planted while he extends. The toe is a target.

- [Keep fencing](#h-p1b)

## The Beat {#h-beat}
@points you 3 Beat attacks to the arm
@points them 1 Counter to your mask
@position Period 1 · long distance

You close to just outside his point, then snap your blade against his, a short sharp *beat* that knocks
his point off line, and in the same movement you extend to his forearm. Red lamp. He looks at his arm,
surprised.

You do it twice more before he adjusts, and he only catches you once, when you beat too wide and he
simply hits your mask.

:::coach
A point in line is a wall, and a beat is a door in it. Knock his point away and for a moment there's
nothing between your point and his arm.
:::

@learn LINE | Henrik holds his point in line. Take his blade (a beat, or better a bind) and his [[counterattack]] disappears.

- [Keep fencing](#h-p1b)

## Waiting {#h-wait}
@penalty you P-yellow card
@penalty them P-yellow card
@position Period 1 · long distance

You wait. Henrik waits. You wait some more. Somebody in the stands coughs. A full minute goes by with
two fencers standing still, two metres apart.

The referee halts the bout and shows you both a yellow card with a P on it.

:::rule
A minute without real fencing is *non-combativity*. The P-card goes to the fencer who is behind, or to
both when the score is level. Collect enough and they turn into penalty touches.
:::

@learn Henrik will not attack first. He's perfectly happy to wait for a minute; you have to make something happen.

- [Keep fencing](#h-p1b)

## Flèche {#h-fleche}
@points them 3 Stop-hits on your flèche
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You launch off your front foot and run at him, arm extended. Henrik takes one long [[step back]]. Your [[flèche]]
was built for someone standing still, and now you're running into empty air with your arm out, straight
onto his point. His lamp lights up while you're still in the air, and you go past him with your blade
nowhere near anything.

You try it twice more. Same result, both times.

:::coach
A flèche commits your whole body. Against a tall, patient fencer who retreats, it just makes you run
onto his point.
:::

@learn Flèche at Henrik from middle [[distance]] and he steps back and [[stop-hits]] you while you're still in the air.

- [To the break](#h-break)

## Pushing Him Back {#h-push}
@points you 1 Double
@points them 1 Double
@position Period 1 · his warning zone

You walk him down the piste, one step at a time, point threatening his hand so he has to keep [[retreating]].
He glances down as his back foot reaches the warning zone, the last two metres before his end line.

Then the heron changes. With nowhere left to go, he stops waiting and launches a long, committed [[lunge]]. It
surprises you so much you just [[counterattack]], and both lamps flash: double.

But you saw it. At the back of the piste, he attacks. One big, predictable lunge.

@learn BACK | At the back of the piste Henrik stops waiting and launches one long, committed lunge. Parry it and riposte.

- [To the break](#h-break)

## The Same Beat {#h-rebeat}
@points them 3 Deceived your beat
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You beat his blade the same way as before. Or you try to: his point simply isn't there. He dropped it
under your beat in a tiny circle, a *dérobement*, and while your blade sweeps through empty air he places
his point on your forearm. Then he does it again, and again.

@learn Beat Henrik's blade the same way twice and he'll deceive it. Vary it, or take the blade with a bind instead.

- [To the break](#h-break)

## The Bind {#h-bind}
@points you 4 Binds to the arm
@position Period 1 · middle distance

This time you don't hit his blade, you take it. Your blade slides onto his, you carry it round in a
circle and down, keeping contact the whole time so he can't slip away, and your point slides off the end
of his blade onto his forearm. Red lamp. He tries to pull his blade free the next time, and you're
already on his arm.

:::coach
A bind (*liement*) is a *prise de fer*: you take possession of his blade instead of knocking it away. A
point-in-line fencer can deceive a beat. It's much harder to deceive a blade that won't let go.
:::

- [To the break](#h-break)

## The Toe {#h-toe1}
@points you 3 Front foot
@points them 1 Wrist
@position Period 1 · middle distance

You wait at the edge of [[distance]]. His front foot slides forward, and plants. Before his arm finishes
extending, you drop your point low and fast onto his toe. Red lamp. The referee nods.

He tries to take your wrist the next time too, and you do it again. He gets your wrist once when you go
too early, but now he's glancing at his own feet.

:::coach
In épée the whole body is target, and a front foot is closer to you than his chest. Toe touches reward a
fencer who watches where the other one puts his weight.
:::

- [To the break](#h-break)

## The Break {#h-break}
@position One-minute break

The buzzer. You walk to the end of the piste, unmask and take a drink. Maître Colette doesn't say a
word for thirty seconds. Then she looks at the scoreboard, at Henrik, and back at you.

"He has changed nothing," she says. "So he thinks he is winning. Next period he will come to you a
little, to steal [[distance]]. When a heron steps, he is standing on one leg."

## Counterattacks {#h-p2-counter}
@points you 2 Doubles
@points them 2 Doubles
@position Period 2 · middle distance

You counter to his wrist as he steps. He's [[counterattacking]] too, and nobody is first by a twenty-fifth
of a second: double, double. Both lamps, both scores.

:::coach
Doubles are arithmetic. When you're ahead they're fine: the gap stays the same and you get closer to 15.
When you're behind, every double is time you don't have.
:::

@learn Counterattacks against Henrik mostly end in doubles. Good when you're ahead, poison when you're behind.

## Into the Step {#h-prep}
@points you 4 Attacks into his preparation
@points them 1 Remise
@position Period 2 · middle distance

You wait for his front foot to lift, and *go*. While his foot is in the air he can't [[retreat]] and his arm
is still bent. Your point lands on his forearm before his foot does. Four times this period you catch
him stepping; once he remises on your mask as you recover.

:::coach
An attack into his *preparation* hits him while he's busy getting ready to do something else. A fencer
who steps forward to steal [[distance]] gives you that moment every single time.
:::

- [Period three](#h-p3)

## The Toe Again {#h-toe2}

@position Period 2 · middle distance

His steps forward make it even easier. Every step ends with a planted front foot, and you're waiting for
it. Toe. Toe. He adjusts and pulls the foot back, and you hit the wrist that's reaching out behind it.
Then the toe again.

He shakes his head inside his mask.

## Retreating {#h-retreat}
@points them 3 Rear-line touch and a stop-hit
@position Period 2 · your end of the piste

You keep backing off, waiting for something better. It doesn't come. You feel the warning zone
under your back foot, then Henrik takes one more step, you take one more back, and both your heels are over
the end line.

The referee halts and gives him the touch. Then he [[stop-hits]] you twice on the restarts for good measure.

:::rule
Cross the rear limit of the piste with both feet and your opponent scores a touch.
:::

@learn Don't [[retreat]] blindly against Henrik. He'll walk you off the end of the piste for a free touch.

## The Heron's Lunge {#h-backparry}
@points you 5 Parry-ripostes
@position Period 3 · his warning zone

You walk him back again. His heel reaches the warning zone and you *feel* it coming: the long [[lunge]],
arm fully extended, everything committed. You're ready this time. You parry, sweeping his blade out of
line with yours, and riposte straight to his chest while he's still stretched out on the lunge.

He comes back to the centre, and you walk him down again. Again he lunges, again you parry.
