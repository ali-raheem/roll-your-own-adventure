## Double Guard Pull {#p-doublepull}
@penalty you Stalling in a double guard pull
@penalty them Stalling in a double guard pull
@position Standing · restart

You grab his sleeve and sit. So does he, at the same moment. You end up facing each other on your
backsides, feet tangled on each other's hips, both of you gripping sleeves and neither of you wanting to
come up on top.

The referee counts out loud. Nobody moves. At the end of the count he stands you both up and gives
each of you a penalty.

:::rule
In a double guard pull, the first athlete to come up on top earns an advantage. If nobody comes up
within the referee's count (twenty seconds under IBJJF rules), both athletes are penalised and restarted
standing.
:::

@learn Tomás pulls guard no matter what. Pulling first just gets you both penalised; you'll have to deal with his guard from the top.

## The Single Leg {#p-single}
@position Closed guard · top

His hand touches your collar and his weight starts to drop as he sits. You drop with him, shoot your
head to his hip and scoop his right leg at the knee. He sits into guard anyway, while you're still
holding his leg.

The referee's hand comes up: **two fingers**. Takedown.

:::rule
If a player pulls guard while their opponent is holding a [[single leg]], the pull is scored as a takedown
for the player holding the leg.
:::

The price: you land between his legs, and they lock around your waist like a car seatbelt.

@points you 2 Takedown
@learn Tomás pulls guard the instant he gets a grip. Catch a leg as he sits and the takedown points are yours.

## Hands on His Chest {#p-lapels}
@position Closed guard · posture broken

You grab both lapels and push down on his chest. It feels strong. It isn't.

With your arms extended and your hands at his chest, your elbows have floated away from your body. He
jerks his knees to his chest, your posture collapses forward, and his right hand slides deep into your
collar while your arms are busy holding up your own bodyweight.

:::coach
Hands on his chest means straight arms and elbows away from your ribs. That's the entry to [[armbars]] and
triangles, and it doesn't stop him pulling your head down at all. Hands on his hips, elbows in.
:::

## Cross Collar {#p-collarchoke}
@position Closed guard · top

You rip your head up and posture. He lets you. The grip stays exactly where it is, because posturing
doesn't remove a grip, it just makes you taller. While both your hands are busy on his belt, his left
hand goes in under your chin into your other collar, palm up.

He crosses his forearms, pulls his elbows wide and drags you down to his chest. The lapels close on
both sides of your neck. The lights go grey at the edges.

You tap.

@lose Submission: cross collar choke

## Strip the Grip {#p-strip}
@position Closed guard · top

Both your hands go to his right wrist. You push it toward his hip, peel it downward and out of your
collar, and pin it to his belly for a moment. *Then* you posture: hips forward, head up, back straight,
both hands on his belt.

@learn COLLAR | Tomás wants a deep cross-collar grip before anything else. Strip it with both hands first, or control his wrist as he pulls guard so it never lands.

:::coach
Grips first, posture second, pass third. In that order, every time.
:::

## Posture {#p-posture}
@position Closed guard · top

Hips forward, back straight, head up, both hands on his hips, elbows tight to your ribs. From down there
he can't reach your collar without sitting up, and every time he sits up you push him back down.

He changes plans. His legs climb higher up your back. He grabs your right sleeve and starts fishing his
left leg up toward your shoulder, looking for an overhook. You know that shape: [[omoplata]], or [[triangle]],
or both.

## Shoulder Lock {#p-omo-tap}
@position Omoplata · defending

## Roll Through {#p-omo-roll}
@position Closed guard · top

You tuck your chin and roll forward over your trapped shoulder, following the direction he's cranking
it. The pressure disappears; your arm comes out as you come over. Tomás scrambles after you, but by the
time you're both upright you're back between his legs, and he's already locking his ankles again.

:::coach
The forward roll relieves an [[omoplata]] because you move with the pressure instead of against it. You're
back in his guard, but your shoulder still works.
:::

## The Stack {#p-tri-stack}

shoulders. Folded in half, he can't get the angle to lock his figure four. You drag your trapped elbow

He lets go and closes his guard again, relaxed as ever. You've survived, and you've learned something.

## Breaking Grips {#p-gripbreak}
@position Open guard · top

You circle your right hand inward, drop the elbow and snap your wrist back toward your hip. The grip
tears free. He reaches for it again, and while he does you do the same with your left.

With his grips gone, his feet slip off your biceps. You grab his pants at both knees.

:::coach
[[Spider guard]] is only as strong as the sleeve grips. Break a grip by rotating your wrist against his
thumb, the weakest part of any grip, and pulling your elbow back to your body.
:::

## Armbar {#p-armbar}
@position Armbar

His arm is straight and pushing on your knee. You grip it to your chest, step your far leg around his
head, and sit down, hips tight to his shoulder, knees pinched. He tries to turn his thumb and hitchhike
out, but you're already lifting your hips.

@win submission: armbar from knee on belly

:::coach
Knee on belly makes people push, and people who push give you straight arms. You didn't beat his guard:
you refused to play in it, and when you finally passed, you made him pay for every mistake on the
bottom he'd never had to make before.
:::
