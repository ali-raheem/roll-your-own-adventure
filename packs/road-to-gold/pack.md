## The Harbour Open

The regional open is small this year, so the organisers have merged the thin brackets: the **Adult
Absolute** is blue and purple belts together, any weight, gi only. You are a two-stripe blue belt,
around 80 kg, with a decent closed guard, a reliable back take and a coach who keeps telling you to
*stop wrestling people who are better at wrestling than you*.

Three wins and the gold is yours. Nobody here is a pushover.

### The ruleset

The event runs IBJJF-style points and blue/purple submission rules.

- **Takedown** 2 · **Sweep** 2 · **Knee on belly** 2 · **Guard pass** 3 · **Mount** 4 · **Back control** (both hooks in) 4
- Positions must be held for about three seconds to score.
- **Advantages** are awarded for near-scores and near-submissions. **Penalties** are given for stalling and for pulling guard without a grip. At this event penalties never turn into points for your opponent; they are tallied and used to break ties.
- At the buzzer: most points wins; if level, most advantages; if still level, fewest penalties; after that it is the referee's call.
- Legal submissions at blue/purple include chokes, straight armlocks, shoulder locks (kimura, americana, omoplata), wrist locks and the **straight ankle lock**. Heel hooks, knee reaping, toe holds, knee bars, calf and bicep slicers, spinal locks without a choke, neck cranks and slams are **illegal**.
- Matches are six minutes.

### How this works

You will lose. A lot, probably. Every loss writes something into your **scouting report**, and some of
those notes are **codewords** that unlock smarter options the next time you face that opponent. Watch the
scoreboard: sometimes the smart play is not a submission, it is being ahead when the buzzer goes.

Everything that happens in this pack is meant to be realistic. If a choice feels like it should work and
it doesn't, the text will tell you why. That is the point.
